# Property Sales

## Purpose
Support property-sale discovery and coordination without representing SWAPY as the closing authority or implying that an in-app signature transfers ownership.

## Eligibility and Listing
- Enable sale listings and binding offers only for an Approved and Enabled property-sale location profile.
- Complete required identity and authority-to-sell checks, listing review, disclosures, property identifiers, and documents configured in that profile.
- Show exact labels for what SWAPY verified and what remains unverified. Keep exact addresses and title/deed/identity documents private to authorized parties and reviewers.

## Sale Flow
1. Buyer reviews the type-labelled listing and submits a non-binding inquiry or offer.
2. Parties negotiate price, included items, contingencies, deadlines, and other terms in versioned records.
3. The platform prompts the locally appropriate due-diligence and closing steps from the approved profile. Depending on that profile, steps may involve qualified professionals, official records, inspections, financing, disclosures, or other required evidence. This is an adaptable checklist, not a universal legal list.
4. Parties use the agreement and signing process explicitly approved for the location. Do not reuse the Items Swap Contract.
5. Payments, deposits, closing funds, and refunds remain disabled until an approved provider and location flow exist.
6. Collect the required closing evidence defined by the profile and route it to the assigned reviewer.
7. Mark **Sale complete** only after all required closing evidence is reviewed and accepted. An in-app signature, payment receipt, or user assertion alone is insufficient.

## Failed or Partial Closing
If due diligence or closing fails, keep the case open under the location profile’s cancellation/refund process. Do not imply SWAPY can unwind a transfer that has legally completed. Preserve evidence and escalation decisions.

## Justification
Property ownership changes require transaction-specific due diligence and completion evidence. Separating coordination from legal closing prevents a platform signature from being mistaken for a completed title transfer.
