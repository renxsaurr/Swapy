# Product and Brand Decisions

## Purpose
Define what SWAPY is, how its modules fit together, and the rules that apply across the marketplace.

## Product Definition
SWAPY is a peer-to-peer marketplace where people sell and trade items, arrange home swaps, offer services, and discover temporary local Garage Sales.

**Brand promise:** Turn what you have into what you want.

## Entry and Discovery
See [[02 - First Visit and Onboarding|First Visit and Onboarding]] and [[03 - Cross-Module Explore and Search|Cross-Module Explore and Search]].

## Modules
- **Items:** physical goods offered for sale, swap, or item plus cash.
- **Homes:** home swaps/stays for agreed dates. Launch scope excludes property sales and ordinary rentals.
- **Services:** bookable services with defined deliverables, schedule, and terms; providers choose accepted payment/trade formats.
- **Garage Sales:** time-bounded local events with their own item inventory and fast in-person handovers.
- **My Store:** a person’s shareable storefront across their listings and events plus private management tools.

## Brand
- Product name: **SWAPY**, matching the provided logo.
- Primary color: **Orange #E87820**.
- Secondary color: teal from the provided logo; confirm exact value from the original logo asset if available.
- Personality: neighborly, practical, optimistic, and trustworthy.
- Tone: clear and friendly. Never imply guaranteed safety, appraisal, or sale.

## Shared Product Rules
1. A listing type determines its transaction flow; offer formats are not the same as modules.
2. Item values are user-provided and unverified. Users decide whether a swap works for them.
3. The current structured offer, signed terms, and transaction timeline must agree.
4. Messaging or sending an offer alone does not reserve an item.
5. Public pages show only information the owner chose to publish. Hide private addresses and access instructions.
6. Report, block, and support tools are available to all users.
7. Sponsored placement is labeled and cannot bypass safety checks or user filters.
8. Payment, deposit, and refund claims must match the approved provider flow and local rules.

## Shared Transaction Pattern
Discover → inspect details → make request/offer → negotiate structured terms → confirm/sign → pay if applicable → hand over/deliver → inspect/confirm → close or resolve a claim → review.

## Decisions Still Requiring Validation
- Exact launch country, currencies, consumer terms, tax obligations, and provider eligibility.
- Home-swap insurance, deposits, and any permitted cost-sharing.
- Final membership prices, listing caps, and promotion credit amounts.
- Provider-approved processing and payout timing for cash components.

## Justification
Separating module type from deal format prevents a user from assuming that every category supports the same transaction. Shared rules create a consistent SWAPY experience, while category-specific flows handle the different risks of shipping, services, events, and access to a home.
